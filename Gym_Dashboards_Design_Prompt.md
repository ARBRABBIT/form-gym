# Design prompt for the gym member and trainer dashboards

Act as a senior mobile product designer. Design two connected, mobile-first dashboards for a gym journal app. The member dashboard helps the gym user set goals, track habits, and log workouts, nutrition, and progress. The trainer dashboard lets the trainer assign and update a member’s workouts and review the workout the member actually logged. Deliver a clickable prototype of both roles using one shared Helvetica-based, black-and-white design system.

## Source material and scope

Use the supplied journal photos, the written requirements, and any verified details from the trainer recording. Use the field inventory below to check the design against every unique journal page. Repeated photos of the same template describe one reusable screen. If something is unclear, preserve the visible label and flag the ambiguity before adding a product rule or permission. Keep the design within the documented workflows.

## Member onboarding

Use three steps with a visible step indicator, Back and Continue controls, and Finish on the last step. Preserve answers when moving between steps. Surface each saved answer in its matching dashboard section.

1. **Month and goals.** Ask the member to select Month and Year and answer “What are your most important goals this month?” Provide four individually labeled text fields, Goal 1 through Goal 4. Show the selected month’s Sunday-to-Saturday calendar, with a way to open and write in each date cell. The member can return to the full planner after onboarding.
2. **Habits.** Ask “What habits do you want to track?” Use repeatable blank habit-name fields. Use the selected month for the initial tracker. Keep logging the daily checkmarks on the Habit Tracker screen.
3. **My Starting Point.** Capture Name, starting Weight, Height, and My Goal with the options Lose weight, Maintain weight, and Gain weight. Keep this weight-goal choice distinct from the four monthly goals. Provide access to the Before area and the baseline measurement fields listed below. Open the dashboard after Finish, with After and comparison data available as the member records results.

## Member dashboard

Put the selected date and the assigned workout near the top, with one clear action to view the assignment or log the workout. Provide direct routes to the monthly planner, habit tracker, 100-day challenge, weight tracker, Starting Point and Results, monthly review, and daily nutrition and wellness log. Use existing journal values when summarizing the member’s entries. Keep full forms on dedicated screens so each remains usable on a phone.

## Trainer dashboard and two-way workout flow

- **Trainer home.** Show the trainer’s linked members. Tapping a member opens that member’s workout view with their planned workouts and submitted logs accessible by date. Treat access to other journal sections and progress photos as a product decision that requires confirmation.
- **Assign a workout.** Select the member and workout date. Use the applicable workout fields to prescribe muscle group, warm-up, exercises, planned reps and weights for Set 1 through Set 6, cool-down, cardio, and instructions using the existing Notes field. Save the workout and show confirmation.
- **Trainer → member.** The assigned workout appears on the matching member’s dashboard for the assigned date. When the trainer edits and saves it, show the latest saved plan in the member app. Show which plan was assigned and which values the member logged through distinct labels.
- **Member → trainer.** The member opens the plan, records actual start and finish, total time, exercises and sets, cardio, notes, and workout satisfaction, then saves the workout log. Make the saved log available from the same assignment in the trainer’s view.
- **State and permissions.** Keep the trainer’s plan and the member’s actual log linked as separate records. A plan edit must preserve member entries and submitted logs. In an implementation, use shared, role-scoped persisted data so the flow works across devices and after reopening. In the prototype, demonstrate the same link using synchronized component states. Show empty, saving, saved, and retry states where they affect the flow.

## Visual design and interactions

- Use **Helvetica** for titles, labels, controls, and data. Use black text, white surfaces, and a restrained gray scale for dividers, secondary labels, and inactive states.
- Use Apple-inspired mobile patterns such as clear large titles, grouped lists, native-feeling pickers, bottom sheets, generous whitespace, and a clear primary action. Use persistent field labels and distinct selected, focused, disabled, and error treatments.
- Design for narrow phone screens first. Respect safe areas and the on-screen keyboard. Aim for 44-point-equivalent tap targets. Convert wide sheets into stacked fields, expandable groups, or date-driven views. Let larger layouts expand while keeping the same content and actions.
- Animate actions with short, subtle transitions for opening a sheet, expanding a section, marking a habit or challenge day, moving through onboarding, and confirming a save. Use a tick, fill, or text label to communicate state in monochrome. Support reduced motion and a readable text scale.

## Deliverables and review

1. Two phone-sized dashboard designs and a shared set of type, spacing, form, list, calendar, and interaction components.
2. All three onboarding screens and the detail or entry screens that contain the journal fields above.
3. A walkthrough showing onboarding → member dashboard and trainer assignment → member receives the workout → trainer changes it → member records the workout → trainer sees the actual log. Demonstrate that a later plan edit preserves the member’s record.
4. A field coverage checklist mapping each journal field and displayed value to its screen. Include the two date fields, all ten measurement rows in both views, all six sets, all four meal sections, and every challenge day.
5. A short list of choices that need confirmation, including the three comparison or target rules and the purpose of the Before and After areas. Keep prototype assumptions explicit in the handoff.
